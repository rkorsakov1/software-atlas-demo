Cloud infrastructure services

InfrastructureScalingBand XL

The Atlas's flagship share series, and the only market where a public time series of vendor share exists. Seven Synergy readings between 2017 and 2026 show two concentration stories at once: the leader's share fell from 34% to 28%, while the big three went 50% - 57% - 63% - 66% - 63% - 63% - 63%, peaking in Q4 2022 and flat ever since (66% is Synergy's own aggregate for that quarter; the named shares here sum to 67 because Amazon ships at the midpoint of the 32-34% band Synergy gave instead of a point). The market became more oligopolistic and less dominated at the same time. 2016, 2018, 2020 and 2023 are absent because no vendor-level share was published or verifiable for them; the series is not interpolated across the gaps.

What is counted

Synergy Research's definition: public infrastructure-as-a-service, platform-as-a-service and hosted private cloud, counted as provider revenue. Gartner's narrower IaaS-only line put the same year at $222B (2025) and $287B (2026F), which is why every size in the Atlas travels with its definition.

Events (8)

  1. March 2006Platform shift

    Amazon launches S3, followed by EC2, and creates cloud infrastructure

    New-market disruption in its purest form: the first customers were startups that enterprise IT did not serve, and utility pricing expanded demand faster than it cut margins.

    AmazonCloud infrastructure servicesS62, S63
  2. February 2010Launch

    Windows Azure reaches general availability

    Four years behind AWS, but attached to every enterprise agreement and identity directory Microsoft already owned, which is what let it close the gap.

    MicrosoftCloud infrastructure servicesApplication platform services (PaaS)S71
  3. June 2014Launch

    Google open-sources Kubernetes

    Open source used as a competitive weapon: commoditizing container orchestration reduced the leader's proprietary lock-in and gave the challenger a standard to compete on.

    GoogleApplication platform services (PaaS)Cloud infrastructure servicesS69
  4. November 2021Spin-off

    IBM separates its managed-infrastructure business as Kyndryl

    IBM cut loose the labour-intensive half of its services business to reweight the company towards hybrid cloud and software. It also breaks IBM's revenue series: FY2020 was filed at $73.620B on the pre-spin basis, FY2021 at $57.350B on the post-spin basis, and IBM's own pro forma restated FY2020 to $57.870B on the later basis — so the apparent 22% fall is a divestiture, not a collapse in demand.

    IBMIT service managementCloud infrastructure servicesS80, S81
  5. June 2026Pricing shift

    Oracle reports $638B of remaining performance obligations

    A backlog nearly ten times annual revenue, built on multi-year AI capacity contracts with a handful of counterparties: simultaneously the strongest growth signal and the largest concentration risk in the dataset.

    OracleCloud infrastructure servicesGPU and AI capacity cloudS13, S13b
  6. July 2026Milestone

    Azure passes $100B of annual revenue

    Microsoft reported FY2026 revenue of $331.8B with Azure above $100B and growing 41%, completing the most successful incumbent platform pivot in the dataset.

    MicrosoftCloud infrastructure servicesS11, S11b
  7. July 2026Milestone

    Cloud infrastructure grows 43% in a quarter, its fastest in eight years

    Twenty years in, the largest infrastructure market in software re-accelerated because AI training and inference demand arrived on top of a base that was already growing.
