Operating systems

InfrastructureMatureBand M

The original control point of the industry, and the clearest case of value migrating away from a layer that still matters technically. Open source drove the server side to near-zero price while the strategic value moved to whoever operated the machines.

What is counted

Licensed and subscription revenue for client, server and mobile operating systems, including enterprise Linux subscriptions. Free operating systems bundled with hardware are counted at zero even where their installed base is enormous.

Events (15)

  1. January 1969Regulation

    US Department of Justice files its antitrust suit against IBM

    Dropped in 1982 without a judgment, but the threat shaped IBM's conduct for thirteen years and is widely credited as a proximate cause of the 1969 unbundling decision.

    IBMOperating systemsB01
  2. June 1969Unbundling

    IBM announces it will price software and services separately from hardware

    Created the commercial software products industry: once the dominant platform charged for software, independent vendors could compete against a priced product rather than a free one. Every later unbundling in this dataset repeats the mechanism.

    IBMOperating systemsDatabasesB03, B01
  3. August 1981Platform shift

    The IBM PC ships with an open architecture and a licensed MS-DOS

    IBM kept the brand and lost the platform: because Microsoft could license DOS to clone makers, the operating system rather than the machine became the control point.

    IBMMicrosoftOperating systemsB01
  4. January 1984Platform shift

    Apple ships the Macintosh with a graphical user interface

    Made graphical interfaces the expectation and created desktop publishing, the first market where creative professionals chose the platform their tools ran on.

    AppleOperating systemsDesign and creative softwareB01
  5. 1985Launch

    Microsoft ships the first version of Windows

    Unremarkable on release, but it started the graphical transition that competitors were late to and that decided the application wars of the early 1990s.

    MicrosoftOperating systemsB01
  6. 1989Licence change

    The GNU General Public License version 1 is published

    Made copyleft a viable commercial force: software could be free to use and still impose obligations, which is the legal basis of every open-source business model that followed.

    Server operating systems and enterprise LinuxOperating systemsB01
  7. September 1991Launch

    The first Linux kernel is released

    Began the commoditization of the server operating system, without which neither hyperscale cloud economics nor low-cost SaaS hosting would have been possible.

    Server operating systems and enterprise LinuxOperating systemsB01
  8. 1993Platform shift

    Mosaic makes the web usable and the browser becomes a platform

    Raised the possibility that applications would run in a browser rather than on an operating system, a threat that drove Microsoft's conduct for the rest of the decade.

    NetscapeOperating systemsB01
  9. August 1995Platform shift

    Windows 95 ships and consolidates the desktop platform

    Settled the desktop question for a decade and gave Microsoft a distribution channel into which it could place any adjacent product at a price of zero.

    MicrosoftOperating systemsB01
  10. 1996Bundling

    Microsoft bundles Internet Explorer with Windows at no additional charge

    Platform envelopment in its purest form: a product with a price competing against the same product at zero, distributed by the operating system every PC shipped with.

    MicrosoftNetscapeOperating systemsB06, B07
  11. 1998Acquisitioncompleted

    Compaq acquires Digital Equipment Corporation

    The end of the minicomputer era: the company that defined the second era of software was absorbed by a PC maker built on the platform that displaced it.

    CompaqDigital Equipment CorporationOperating systemsB01
  12. May 1998Regulation

    United States v. Microsoft is filed

    The case that defined the limits of bundling by a dominant platform and constrained Microsoft's conduct through the years when Google and Firefox were establishing themselves.

    MicrosoftNetscapeOperating systemsB06
  13. April 2000Regulation

    District court finds Microsoft liable for monopoly maintenance and orders a breakup

    The only time a US court ordered the breakup of a major software company; the remedy was reversed on appeal a year later.

    MicrosoftOperating systemsB06
  14. June 2001Regulation

    The D.C. Circuit upholds monopoly-maintenance liability but vacates the breakup

    Established the modern US standard for platform tying and set the expectation that remedies would be behavioural rather than structural.

    MicrosoftOperating systemsB06
  15. November 2002Regulation

    The US v. Microsoft settlement imposes conduct remedies

    Interoperability disclosure and OEM freedom rather than divestiture. Whether that was sufficient is the question every subsequent platform case has re-litigated.

    MicrosoftOperating systemsB06
